Steven built his career in energy — starting in chemicals and refining manufacturing, then moving into trading and risk management across crude, refined products, natural gas, and electricity, in the US and internationally. He has operated at every scale, from integrated majors to PE-backed platforms to early-stage startups. That range shapes how he reads macro data: not as an academic exercise, but as something that shows up directly in demand forecasts, financing costs, and the assumptions behind a deal thesis.

A significant part of his career has been in retail energy, regulated and deregulated — including righting the ship at Direct Energy Residential, the largest deregulated energy retailer in North America. In all, he has led seven successful turnarounds in the last fifteen years, spanning retail energy, steel, chemicals, upstream, and recycling. His management style favors swift, decisive change built on strong teams — several people he's worked with have chosen to join him more than once.

Today he works with private equity firms and portfolio company leadership teams, translating macroeconomic conditions — rate cycles, credit availability, labor markets, sector-specific demand shifts — into implications for underwriting, operating plans, and board-level decisions, mostly in the energy and commodity space. Engagements range from a single investment committee briefing to ongoing advisory retainers.

His board and advisory work has included several non-executive director roles — a public company's emergence from bankruptcy, a fast-growing credit union, and the NACD TriCity Chapter board — alongside helping shepherd an energy-sector startup building next-generation usage-forecasting and customer-segmentation software.

He writes the Insights section for the same reason he takes on advisory work: most macro commentary is either too academic to act on or too shallow to trust. His aim is the middle — analysis a management team or deal team can actually use.
